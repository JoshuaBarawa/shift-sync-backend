Constraint Engine (8 checks on every assignment)
-------------------------------------------------

When a manager assigns a staff member to a shift, the system runs these checks
in order and blocks on the first failure with a clear explanation:

1. Location certification  — Is the staff member certified to work at this location?
2. Skill match             — Does the staff member have the required skill for this shift?
3. Availability            — Is the staff member available during these hours on this day?
4. Double booking          — Is the staff member already assigned to another shift on this date?
5. 10-hour rest rule       — Is there at least 10 hours between the end of their previous/next shift?
6. Headcount               — Is the shift already fully staffed?
7. Already assigned        — Is this staff member already on this shift?
8. Daily hours             — Does this shift exceed 12 hours? (hard block)

After all 8 checks pass, a weekly overtime check runs:
  - 35+ hours: assignment proceeds with a warning
  - 40+ hours: hard block, assignment rejected


DECISIONS MADE ON INTENTIONAL AMBIGUITIES
-------------------------------------------

1. What happens to historical data when a staff member is de-certified from a location?

Decision:
Historical assignments are preserved. De-certification only affects future assignments.
If Bob worked at Location A last week and is de-certified today, last week's shift records
remain intact. The constraint engine only checks current certification status at the time
of assignment.

Reason:
Deleting or invalidating historical data would corrupt the audit trail and make
payroll/reporting unreliable. Past assignments were valid when they were made.


2. How should "desired hours" interact with availability windows?

Decision:
They are treated as separate concepts. Availability windows define when a staff member
can work (hard constraint enforced by the system). Desired hours are a soft preference
visible to managers on the staff detail view as a weekly hours target. The system does
not auto-reject assignments that exceed desired hours — it only surfaces the information.

Reason:
Desired hours are a scheduling aid, not a contract. Enforcing them as hard constraints
would create too many edge cases (e.g., staff who set low desired hours but accept
overtime voluntarily).


3. When calculating consecutive days, does a 1-hour shift count the same as an 11-hour shift?

Decision:
Yes. Any shift on a given calendar day counts as a worked day for consecutive day
tracking purposes, regardless of duration.

Reason:
Labor law consecutive day rules are based on calendar days worked, not hours worked.
A 1-hour shift still requires the employee to commute, prepare, and be present —
it counts as a day worked.


4. If a shift is edited after swap approval but before it occurs, what should happen?

Decision:
If a manager edits a shift that has an approved swap, the system automatically cancels
the swap and notifies all parties. The assignment reverts to the state before the swap
was approved. The manager must re-assign and re-publish.

Reason:
An approved swap is an agreement between two staff members based on specific shift
details (time, location, date). Editing the shift changes the terms of that agreement.
Silently keeping the swap active would mean a staff member is bound to a shift they
never agreed to.

Implementation:
cancelPendingSwapsForShift() is called in shifts.service.ts on every update() operation.
It cancels swaps with status pending_acceptance, open, or pending_approval.


5. How should the system handle a location that spans a timezone boundary?

Decision:
Each location has a single canonical timezone stored in the database (location.timezone).
All shift times for that location are displayed and interpreted in that timezone. A staff
member certified at multiple locations sees each shift in the timezone of that shift's location.

Reason:
A restaurant operates on one local time regardless of where the state line sits. Assigning
a timezone per location is the simplest correct model. A location near a state line should
be assigned the timezone where the building physically sits.

Known limitation:
The current implementation stores timezone as a string but does not yet perform full UTC
conversion in the frontend. Times are stored as entered and displayed as entered. Full
timezone conversion is documented as a known gap below.

ASSUMPTIONS MADE
-----------------

- A "week" for overtime calculation purposes runs Monday to Sunday
- Overnight shifts (e.g., 11pm to 3am) are treated as a single shift belonging to the start date
- Staff availability windows apply to local time at the location of the shift
- Manager approval is required for all swaps and drops, including drop pickups
- The 48-hour edit cutoff for published schedules is not enforced in the current build —
  managers can edit at any time (flagged as a known gap)
- "Desired hours" is not currently a field on the user model but is planned
